Abstract

Malombo as ontology, not residue

This archive is based on my final paper arguing that Malombo names both a Venda healing ritual and a modern musical language. In Vhavenda ritual life, malombo organizes possession, drumming, chanting, dance, and the restoration of balance between human beings and ancestral forces. In twentieth-century South Africa, Philip Nchipi Tabane and the Malombo Jazz Makers carried those same structures into urban circuits shaped by apartheid, recording studios, radio, festivals, and anti-apartheid cultural spaces. Rather than reading that movement as a transition from “traditional African music” into jazz modernity, the project treats Tabane’s work as a continuation of ritual logic under new historical conditions. Malombo therefore challenges colonial binaries separating traditional from modern, spiritual from intellectual, and ritual from experimentation. Drawing on Blacking, Davhula, Rouget, Turner, Felber, Erlmann, and Galane, the site argues that sound in Malombo functions not merely as aesthetic expression but as ontology: it reorganizes embodiment, consciousness, ancestry, and social reality.

Archive Note

This version of the archive follows the final paper’s central claim: Malombo is not a leftover tradition modernity leaves behind, but a living epistemology whose ritual structures continue inside urban performance.
